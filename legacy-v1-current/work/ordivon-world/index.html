External interaction · Conditioned evidence · Reconciliation and rebinding

Let a Task reach and act through a changing external world without rebuilding the world beneath it.

Ordivon World unifies the former Link and Edge prototypes around one complete interaction: discover a target or capability, bind the exact path, identity, transport, provider, and authority, execute or hand off the work, preserve Receipt and Artifact provenance, reconcile uncertain outcomes, and continue when any external condition changes.

Question

What must remain continuous when connection and external action fail together?

A real external action cannot be divided cleanly into “connect first” and “act later.” The intended Effect determines endpoint, path, identity, locality, session, and provider requirements. Those conditions can change the observation or consequence. A timeout may mean failed delivery, a lost response, or successful remote execution whose acknowledgement disappeared.

World therefore correlates rather than collapses Task, Effect, Dispatch, delivery, session, provider execution, Receipt, Artifact, and Observation identities.

Interaction

One external history spans relation, path, provider, action, result, and recovery.

01Intent

A Task identifies an external relation, capability, consequence, data, locality, identity, evidence, or callback need.

02Observation

Versioned facts describe targets, endpoints, paths, transports, identities, providers, capabilities, availability, policy, and uncertainty.

03Binding

Exact semantic references are correlated with the selected path, endpoint, provider execution, authority, policy, and observation revisions.

04Interaction

Mature APIs, browsers, Sandboxes, queues, stores, Agents, or devices communicate and act without World reimplementing their native mechanisms.

05Evidence

Receipts, Artifacts, Observations, callbacks, digests, and residual state preserve the conditions under which the result was produced.

06Continuation

Unknown outcomes are reconciled before retry; stale claims are invalidated; path, provider, body, transport, identity, or participant can be rebound.

Topology

The external world is a graph, not a local-to-cloud request pipe.

Local → remote

API calls, Browser execution, Sandbox work, data queries, Agent delegation, or device control.

Remote → local

Callbacks, webhooks, streams, notifications, approvals, and delayed result delivery.

Remote → remote

Direct Artifact transfer, provider chaining, storage-to-compute flows, and external verification without proxying all bytes through Host.

Fan-out and join

One Task can use many providers or participants and later join independently conditioned Artifacts into one verified result.

Boundary

World coordinates external meaning; mature systems still move bytes and run workloads.

Host owns Goal, Task, strategy, participant commitments, and completion. Runtime owns trusted-local Workspaces, Jobs, processes, cancellation, and recovery. Network, identity, cloud, Browser, Sandbox, queue, storage, and device systems retain their native mechanisms and physical lifecycle.

World is not a proxy, VPN, CNI, Service Mesh, Browser platform, Sandbox scheduler, workflow engine, global identity, or universal protocol. Its independent existence remains falsifiable: the research may conclude that Host plus provider and observation adapters is simpler.
