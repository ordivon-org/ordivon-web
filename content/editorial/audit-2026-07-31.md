# R1 Interface Copy Audit — 31 July 2026

Baseline: `origin/main` at `8509023eb37aab74e7659e04c437b40f5f750195`  
Scope: all top-level public pages, dynamic Project and Research templates, article template, navigation, footer, metadata, 404, publication registry, and distribution surfaces.  
Publication set: 19 registered articles, including the H1–H5 Harness closeout published on 31 July 2026.

## Executive judgment

Ordivon Web has a distinctive and credible intellectual voice. It already avoids generic startup language, exposes uncertainty, preserves negative results, and contains several memorable titles and brand lines.

The dominant weakness is not poor prose. It is **an entry-order problem**:

> Internal architecture and content-model language often arrives before the reader understands the practical failure, consequence, or result.

The site currently reads most naturally to someone already participating in Ordivon. A new technical reader can understand it, but must perform unnecessary translation from `state owner`, `Question dossier`, `public model`, `deletion condition`, and capitalized schema objects into ordinary engineering consequences.

A second weakness is voice uniformity. Home, System, Research, Writing, Now, About, and Colophon frequently use the same abstract, corrective, research-governance register even though the pages have different jobs.

A third weakness is distribution packaging. The article system is structurally strong, but all articles share one Open Graph image, author identity remains only `zycxfyh`, and the Writing index prioritizes metadata relationships over editorial recommendation.

## What is already strong

- `Work should survive the intelligence that started it.` is a durable homepage thesis.
- `Persistent work. Recoverable execution. Durable evidence.` is a concise footer position.
- Research copy consistently separates current judgment from exact source truth.
- Project detail pages expose current state, evidence, ownership, hard boundary, open questions, and related writing.
- Article metadata includes title, deck, description, lead, type, date, read time, author, project, questions, and table of contents.
- Flagship reports include real numbers, baselines, contradictions, limitations, and primary records.
- Titles such as `Winning the Move Can Lose the Contest`, `One Authority, Thirteen Tables Deleted`, and `A Transcript Is Not a Task Database` are unusually strong.
- The latest R3–R8 content-model reduction already removed much of the former graph-object excess. The audit should not reverse that work.

## Scoring method

Each surface is scored from 1 to 5 across seven dimensions:

- **Clarity** — a new reader can identify subject and meaning.
- **Stakes** — the copy explains why the subject matters.
- **Specificity** — claims contain actors, actions, results, or boundaries.
- **Evidence** — the surface signals or links the basis of its claims.
- **Voice** — the copy is distinctive, controlled, and consistent with Ordivon.
- **Jargon access** — formal language is introduced progressively; higher is better.
- **Action** — the next useful reader action is clear.

The score is directional, not a content KPI. A technically dense Project page may legitimately score lower on jargon access than a homepage.

## Cross-site findings

### 1. The category is still implicit

The homepage says `Independent agent systems` and immediately introduces Tasks, committed effects, interactions, and evidence. A first-time reader still has to decide whether Ordivon is a company, framework, research laboratory, portfolio, architecture, or collection of repositories.

Required R2 direction:

- identify Ordivon as independent research and engineering for durable agent work;
- explain the failure in ordinary language before the formal object list;
- retain the current thesis line.

### 2. Negative definition is overused

Recurring structures include:

- not a product diagram;
- not a second fact database;
- no shared fiction;
- not a delivery forecast;
- does not replace the source;
- must not reconstruct.

These boundaries are valid, but their repetition gives the public voice a defensive posture. Lead with the positive mechanism or capability, then state the avoided error.

### 3. Formal capitalization is too common in public summaries

`Tasks`, `Tool-use Run`, `Provider`, `Agent Loop`, `Question`, `Artifact`, and similar terms often appear as formal schema identities before the reader has learned the model.

Required direction:

- use lowercase for generic agent, model, provider, session, task, run, tool, and artifact;
- reserve capitals for exact Ordivon objects;
- rewrite metadata descriptions to minimize schema language.

### 4. Content-model explanations displace reader value

Several pages explain that metadata is derived, no second registry exists, or a diagram is curated. This is useful implementation discipline, but it is frequently above more valuable reader questions:

- What changed?
- What can this system do?
- What did the experiment establish?
- Which article should I read first?

The content model should remain infrastructure, not the lead story.

### 5. Page voices are insufficiently differentiated

- Home should attract and prove.
- System should explain one work trajectory and ownership boundaries.
- Research should foreground active questions and changed judgments.
- Writing should curate, recommend, and frame arguments.
- Now should summarize change in direct language.
- About should provide motive, origin, author identity, and trust.

Current copy often makes all six pages sound like documentation for the publication model.

### 6. Reader value appears after internal accuracy

The copy is usually correct about where facts live. It is less consistent about why an external reader should care. Source-of-truth disclaimers should usually follow the result, not precede it.

## Page-by-page audit

## Home

### Keep

- `Work should survive the intelligence that started it.`
- the three-step session ended / task remained / continuation recovered sequence;
- the separation of proof, ownership, frontier, writing, and final action;
- the closing `Follow the trajectory, not the session.` as a secondary recurring line.

### Problems

- `Independent agent systems` is too broad to establish category.
- The hero description begins with four formal object classes.
- `Four owners. No shared fiction.` is memorable but not self-explanatory.
- The primary CTA `Explore the system` asks for navigation rather than promising value.
- The proof section says task truth survived, but does not surface the strongest recent H1–H5 numbers or both replacement orders.
- The homepage promotes whichever Question has the current metadata count rather than making an explicit editorial choice about the most important current question.

### R2 direction

- Retain the title.
- Add a plain category and consequence-oriented two-sentence explanation.
- Promote one concrete tested result above the architecture map.
- Change CTAs to `Start with the architecture` and `Read the replacement experiment` or equivalent.
- Explain four owners through the four failures they prevent.

## System

### Keep

- reduction to four public object types;
- explicit separation between public views and source repositories;
- state-owner summaries and owns/does-not-own structure.

### Problems

- `Ordivon has a small public model and richer source repositories` describes information architecture, not the system's purpose.
- The first section teaches the publication ontology before showing how one real task crosses the system.
- `Four boundaries prevent convenient fiction` is strong but abstract.
- Public metrics count objects rather than tested capabilities.

### R2 direction

Open with one end-to-end trajectory:

> A model proposes. Host preserves task meaning. Runtime executes locally. World records external consequences. Computing tests the contracts between them.

Then show the object model as a reading aid.

## Research

### Keep

- `Questions are the durable unit of unfinished work.`
- current judgment, next test, and deletion condition;
- explicit repository/source authority;
- dated publication timeline.

### Problems

- the first paragraph asks readers to understand dossier structure before seeing an important question;
- `Question dossier` and `deletion condition` appear above the fold;
- summary metrics count dossiers and project lines rather than closed or changed judgments;
- there is no editorial `Start here` path.

### R2 direction

Lead with three categories:

1. most important question under test;
2. most recently answered question;
3. experiment that most changed the architecture.

Explain the research contract after these examples.

## Projects index

### Keep

- four-project structure;
- project thesis, summary, current state, and ownership;
- portfolio-boundary explanation.

### Problems

- the title `Independent state owners, connected by evidence` assumes the architecture before stating project value;
- every card emphasizes owns/current state but not latest proof, usability, maturity, or intended reader;
- the System map repeats the same abstraction before individual project relevance is established.

### R2 direction

Each card should answer:

- problem;
- what the project does;
- current maturity;
- latest proof;
- open question;
- repository or flagship article.

Ownership remains on the detail page.

## Project detail template

### Keep

This is the strongest general template. It exposes thesis, summary, repository, central question, mechanism, current state, evidence, boundaries, open questions, and related writing.

### Problems

- the hero uses the project thesis as the `h1`; some theses are meaningful only after project context;
- `Facts this project is allowed to commit` is precise but legalistic;
- metric quality depends on project metadata and may drift toward internal counts;
- no explicit maturity or `Who this is for` field.

### R2 direction

Tune labels rather than redesign the template. Add a compact maturity/use statement and ensure every evidence metric demonstrates capability, comparison, or release state.

## Writing index

### Keep

- chronological archive;
- publication types and read time;
- Question-derived topics;
- publishing rule against filling pages.

### Problems

- `Dated arguments, not a second fact database` leads with internal editorial governance;
- readers receive no flagship recommendation or reading path;
- topics begin from research Question metadata, which may be too formal for general readers;
- no visible separation between essays, experiments, architecture decisions, release notes, and short findings beyond filters;
- the page explains relation derivation (`No separate relation registry`) instead of why the topic matters.

### R2 direction

New hierarchy:

1. one flagship publication;
2. `Start here` reading path;
3. current reports;
4. thematic series;
5. complete archive;
6. optional Question-based advanced navigation.

## Article template

### Keep

- title, deck, author, date, read time, TOC, lead;
- complete MDX content;
- related Project and Question context;
- adjacent publication navigation;
- schema.org Article metadata.

### Problems

- article metadata displays research Question count or project-area count, which is implementation context rather than reader value;
- every article shares the same social image;
- `zycxfyh` is a valid handle but does not establish a durable author identity or author page;
- `Where this article sits` and metadata-derived links are accurate but visually larger than their editorial value;
- article types share one template even when a report, release, note, and essay need different reading aids;
- no consistent `In brief`, key findings, evidence level, or limitation summary at the template level.

### R2/R4 direction

- replace graph/context counts with evidence or update status where useful;
- add distinct social images;
- establish an author presentation without inventing an organization;
- reduce publication-context prominence;
- later introduce type-specific MDX components.

## Now

### Keep

- current questions, recent publications, project boundaries, and revised judgments are all useful ingredients;
- the page is derived from canonical content rather than a second event database.

### Problems

- `The frontier is what could still change the map` is abstract and does not say what changed now;
- the supporting paragraph foregrounds implementation derivation;
- the page contains four data views but no editorial synthesis;
- `Current` in metadata/navigation conflicts with `/now`;
- recent publication cards may dominate even when the important change was deletion, release, or failed experiment.

### R2 direction

Use the direct frame:

> What changed, what we learned, what we removed, and what we are testing now.

Add a short authored summary at the top. Keep generated sections below it.

## About

### Keep

- independence is explicit;
- source-of-truth discipline is clear;
- working method is credible;
- GitHub is the primary technical contact route.

### Problems

- no origin story or concrete initial failure;
- no explanation of why one independent maintainer is pursuing this system;
- no long-term ambition beyond publication organization;
- `without pretending they form a finished company or fixed theory` again leads through negation;
- `zycxfyh` remains an account identifier rather than an explained author identity.

### R2 direction

Add:

- how Ordivon began;
- the recurring failure that connected the projects;
- the engineering principles that emerged through use;
- the long-term ambition;
- author identity and public working mode;
- clear invitation for technical critique or contribution.

## Colophon

### Keep

The page is precise, proportionate, and appropriately technical. It directly explains the publication contract, static delivery, recovery, and why machinery was removed.

### Minor issue

The hero still uses a defensive contrast. This is acceptable on a Colophon because implementation economy is the subject.

## Navigation and footer

### Findings

- Desktop: System, Research, Writing, Current, GitHub.
- Mobile additionally exposes Projects and About.
- Footer exposes all pages and RSS.
- The discrepancy makes Projects appear secondary on desktop even though Projects are a primary public object.
- `Current` should become `Now` everywhere.
- The footer tagline should remain unchanged.

### R2 direction

Recommended primary navigation:

`System · Projects · Research · Writing · Now · GitHub`

Keep About and Colophon in the footer. About may remain in mobile navigation if space permits.

## 404

### Current

`404 / Unknown node` and `This route is not part of the current graph.`

### Finding

The internal graph metaphor is clever but assumes the very model that entry copy should avoid. A missing route may belong to a deleted or renamed project, so the recovery path should offer Writing, Projects, and Research, not only Home.

## Metadata and social packaging

### Findings

- page descriptions are technically accurate but frequently use internal terms;
- all 19 articles use `/opengraph-image.png` with the same alt text;
- social previews cannot visually distinguish a game release, security report, acceleration essay, or harness architecture guide;
- descriptions often list formal objects instead of the result and significance;
- no article-specific cover field exists.

### Required direction

- rewrite descriptions as independent public summaries;
- add per-article social image support;
- create visual families by publication type or series;
- use exact dates and author identity;
- preserve canonical and structured data.

## R1 acceptance result

R1 is complete when:

- all primary public surfaces are inventoried;
- every top-level page and template has a scored baseline;
- cross-site terminology and voice problems are identified;
- all registered publication titles are classified;
- R2 priorities are ordered by public leverage rather than repository convenience;

This document satisfies that baseline. The next implementation round should change public copy in small, reviewable groups and evaluate each change against the R0 files rather than performing an undifferentiated full-site rewrite.
